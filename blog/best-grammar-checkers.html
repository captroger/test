Best Grammar Checkers in 2026 — Complete Comparison

Find the right grammar tool for your writing style, budget, and workflow.

But with so many options available, how do you choose? In this guide, we compare the top five grammar checkers of 2026 across features, pricing, accuracy, and ideal use cases.

1. Grammarly — The All-Round Champion

Grammarly is the most popular grammar checker in the world for a reason: it just works. The browser extension seamlessly checks your writing across Gmail, Google Docs, LinkedIn, Twitter, and virtually every text field on the web.

The free version covers spelling, basic grammar, and punctuation — enough for casual writers. The Premium plan ($12/month billed annually) adds tone detection, clarity-focused sentence rewrites, word choice suggestions, and a plagiarism checker that scans billions of web pages. For teams, Grammarly Business adds style guides, brand tones, and analytics at $15/member/month.

Who should use it: Professionals, students, job seekers, and anyone who writes across multiple platforms daily. Grammarly is the best choice if you want one tool that works everywhere.

Standout feature: The tone detector tells you how your message will come across (friendly, formal, confident, etc.) — enormously useful for business email.

2. ProWritingAid — The Writer's Deep-Dive Tool

ProWritingAid is not just a grammar checker — it is a comprehensive writing coach. It generates over 20 different writing reports covering readability, sentence variety, passive voice, dialogue tags, pacing, alliteration, cliché detection, and more. If Grammarly is a helpful editor, ProWritingAid is a writing seminar.

It integrates directly with Scrivener — making it the go-to choice for novelists. The free version checks up to 500 words at a time; the Premium plan ($10/month annually) removes that limit and unlocks all reports. A lifetime purchase option is available for $399 — a great deal for long-term users.

Who should use it: Novelists, academic writers, and anyone who wants to understand why their writing works (or doesn't). The learning curve is steeper than Grammarly, but the depth of analysis is unmatched.

Try ProWritingAid Free →

3. Hemingway Editor — Bold, Clear Prose

The Hemingway Editor takes a fundamentally different approach. Instead of checking for grammatical errors, it focuses on readability and clarity. It highlights sentences that are hard to read, flags passive voice, counts adverbs, and assigns a readability grade level to your text.

The free web version at hemingwayapp.com is fully functional — paste your text and edit inline. The desktop app ($19.99 one-time purchase) adds offline access, export to Word/PDF, and direct publishing to WordPress and Medium.

Who should use it: Bloggers, content marketers, and any writer who wants their prose to be punchy and direct. Hemingway pairs beautifully with our TextTally word counter and writing statistics tool for complete text analysis.

4. LanguageTool — Best for Multilingual Writers

LanguageTool supports over 30 languages — far more than Grammarly or ProWritingAid. If you write in Dutch, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, or any language other than English, LanguageTool is your best option. It also powers TextTally's built-in grammar checker for online users.

The free version checks basic grammar and spelling. The Premium plan ($4.99/month) adds style suggestions, advanced punctuation checks, and a higher character limit. It is the most affordable premium grammar tool on this list.

Who should use it: Multilingual writers, European professionals, and anyone who needs grammar checking beyond English. The open-source community version is also available for self-hosting.

5. Ginger — Best for ESL Writers and Rephrasing

Ginger focuses on sentence rephrasing and translation, making it especially useful for English-as-a-second-language (ESL) writers. Its sentence rephraser suggests alternative ways to express the same idea — helpful for avoiding repetitive phrasing. Ginger also includes a built-in translator supporting 40+ languages.

The free version is limited in scope; the Premium plan starts at $20.97/month. While Ginger's grammar checking is not as comprehensive as Grammarly's, its rephrasing and translation features fill a unique niche.

Who should use it: ESL writers, non-native English speakers, and anyone who frequently needs to rephrase sentences for variety.

Which Grammar Checker Should You Choose?

The right tool depends on your specific needs:

  • For everyday writing and seamless integration: Choose Grammarly. Its browser extension and polished interface make it the easiest to adopt.
  • For novelists and deep style analysis: Choose ProWritingAid. The 20+ reports will teach you more about writing than any other tool.
  • For bold, clear, concise prose: Choose Hemingway Editor. It forces you to simplify — and your readers will thank you.
  • For multilingual writing: Choose LanguageTool. No other tool supports as many languages.
  • For ESL writers who need rephrasing help: Choose Ginger. Its rephraser and translator are genuinely useful.

Many writers use two tools in combination: Grammarly for real-time checking plus Hemingway for final readability polish. Pair either with TextTally's word counter to track your word count, reading time, and character counts as you edit.
