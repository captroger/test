How to Improve Your Reading Speed: Tips and Tools

Practical techniques to read faster while maintaining comprehension.

By TextTally Team | Published: July 2026

Reading faster is a skill anyone can learn. Whether you are a student with extensive reading lists, a professional keeping up with industry news, or a lifelong learner, improving your reading speed saves time and boosts productivity. The average adult reads at 200-250 words per minute (WPM), but with practice, you can reach 400 WPM or more.

Measure Your Current Reading Speed

Before you can improve, you need to know your baseline. Use TextTally's reading time calculator to measure your current WPM. Simply paste a text, read it while timing yourself, and divide the word count by the minutes taken. Most people are surprised by how slow they actually read.

Technique 1: Chunking

Instead of reading one word at a time, train your eyes to read groups of 3-4 words simultaneously. This reduces the number of eye movements (saccades) and increases reading speed. Start with small groups and gradually expand.

Technique 2: Minimize Subvocalization

Subvocalization is the habit of silently pronouncing each word as you read. While natural, it limits your reading speed to speaking speed (about 150 WPM). Practice reading without "hearing" the words in your head. Use a pointer or your finger to guide your eyes faster than your inner voice can keep up.

Technique 3: Use a Pointer

Using a finger, pen, or cursor to guide your eyes along the text forces your eyes to move faster and reduces regression (going back to re-read words). This is one of the simplest and most effective speed reading techniques.

Technique 4: Expand Peripheral Vision

Train your peripheral vision to capture more words per line. Instead of focusing on the first and last word of each line, try to see the entire line at once. This takes practice but can dramatically increase reading speed.

Technique 5: Preview and Skim

Before reading a text in detail, preview it by scanning headings, subheadings, and the first sentence of each paragraph. This gives you a mental framework that makes reading faster and improves comprehension.

Track Your Progress

Use TextTally's word counter to track the word count of passages you read. Practice these techniques for 15 minutes daily and measure your WPM weekly. Most people see a 50-100% improvement within a month of consistent practice.
