What Is a Text Case Converter?

A text case converter is a simple but incredibly useful tool that changes the capitalization of your text. Instead of manually retyping or reformatting paragraphs, you can paste any text and convert it to the case format you need with a single click. Whether you're fixing an accidentally caps-locked document, formatting a headline, or creating stylistic effects, a case converter saves time and eliminates errors.

TextTally's case converter supports five common case formats: UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, Sentence case, and aLtErNaTiNg CaSe. All conversions happen entirely in your browser — your text never leaves your device.

Supported Case Formats

  • UPPERCASE: Converts every character to uppercase. Useful for acronyms, headings, or when you need text to stand out.
  • lowercase: Converts every character to lowercase. Perfect for email addresses, URLs, or when you need uniform casing.
  • Title Case: Capitalizes the first letter of each major word. Ideal for headlines, book titles, article headings, and presentation slides.
  • Sentence case: Capitalizes only the first letter of each sentence. The standard format for body text in articles, essays, and emails.
  • aLtErNaTiNg CaSe: Alternates between lowercase and uppercase for a playful or sarcastic tone. Popular in memes and internet culture.

When to Use Each Case Format

Choosing the right case format depends on your context:

  • UPPERCASE — Use sparingly for emphasis, warnings, or acronyms. Too much uppercase can feel like shouting.
  • lowercase — Use for programming identifiers, email addresses, or when a casual, modern aesthetic is desired.
  • Title Case — Use for headlines, blog post titles, book titles, and formal presentation slides. Follows AP Style or Chicago Manual conventions.
  • Sentence case — Use for body text, articles, reports, emails, and most professional writing. This is the default for most content.
  • Alternating Case — Use for humorous or ironic emphasis in social media, memes, and casual messaging.

What is the difference between Title Case and Sentence case?

Title Case capitalizes the first letter of every major word (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs), while Sentence case only capitalizes the first word of each sentence plus proper nouns. Title Case is for headings; Sentence case is for body text.
